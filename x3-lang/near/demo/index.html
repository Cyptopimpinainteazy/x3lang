      What 1Click quoted

      Change any rule, then get a dry quote. Each line of the program gets its verdict in the margin.

      What happens when you press the button

      1. Your program becomes a quote request

        The page compiles the X3 intent in your browser. require slippage becomes 1Click's tolerance in whole basis points, rounded toward the stricter side. timeout becomes the deadline after which 1Click refunds. A rule NEAR Intents cannot enforce is refused, not dropped.

      2. 1Click quotes it, and signs the quote

        The request goes straight from your browser to the 1Click API. 1Click signs every quote with its Ed25519 manager key.

      3. This page checks the signature before reading a number

        It rebuilds the exact bytes 1Click signed, using the same steps as NEAR's official SDK, and verifies them with your browser's own cryptography. A forged or altered quote stops here.

      4. Every rule is checked against the signed numbers

        The amount, the floor, slippage, the cost of the swap and of a refund, and whether the quote is fresh. Each verdict lands on the line of the program it came from.

      5. The demo checks the owner’s mandate

        A program that says under mandate <owner> was written by an agent. Before 1Click is asked for anything, the page checks it against the owner's mandate: who may be paid, in what, how much per payment and per 24 hours, where refunds go, and the loosest slippage and fees allowed. On NEAR, x3-guard runs the same checks when the agent registers and again when it accepts a quote. An owner can revoke the agent’s mandate for later Guard-mediated requests.

      For a real swap, the x3-guard contract does step 3 and step 4 again on NEAR, with env::ed25519_verify, and lets each nonce open only one deposit address. Spending over 24 hours, approvals and revocation live in the contract, so this page checks a mandate as written, with nothing spent yet. It stays on dry quotes, so it never holds keys or moves funds. An unrestricted key with access to the funds can still transfer them directly.

      What each demonstration proves

      • Local policy evaluation: the browser shows how an owner mandate rules out a payment to an unlisted recipient.
      • NEAR testnet Guard decisions: the deployed contract refused a signed 1Click dry quote with a mismatched recipient. A separate accepted quote stopped before deposit.
      • Finalized testnet settlement: a separate transfer sent 1.000000 test USDC on Base Sepolia, and 0.997400 test USDC arrived on NEAR testnet. The route relied on the trusted Tidepool relayer. The receipts show the finalized transfers on each chain and leave cross-chain causality dependent on the relay.
